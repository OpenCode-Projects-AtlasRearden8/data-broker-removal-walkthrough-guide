# The Walkthrough: Finding Yourself on Brokers, Step by Step

*Education only — not legal advice, and no scraping or hammering any website.
Everything below is normal visitor-level activity on each broker's own site.*

## Step 0 — Set up your removal kit (10 minutes)

Before touching any broker site, gather:

- [ ] The **exact name spelling** brokers likely use for you (and any past name)
- [ ] Current + past **cities/states** you've lived in (2–3 is enough)
- [ ] The **last 4 digits only** of anything they might ask to verify — never put a
      full SSN or full ID scan in an email. Reputable brokers only need name + city
      + a URL to your listing.
- [ ] A dedicated **email address just for this project** (a free Gmail/Outlook
      alias). Keeps confirmations and follow-ups in one place; keeps broker spam
      out of your real inbox.
- [ ] A simple **tracking sheet** — the printable checklist in `site/` or a small
      spreadsheet: *broker name, URL of your listing, date requested, method
      (web form / email), confirmation #, date completed.*
- [ ] Calendar reminders, per `06-renewals-calendar.md`.

## Step 1 — Find your listings (30–60 minutes, once)

Search Google (or another engine) for combinations of:

```
"[Your Name]" [City, ST] dui NOT case    ← no, don't overthink it
```

Realistically, these simpler searches find almost everything:

```
"[Your Name]" "[City]"
"[Your Name]" "[Former City]"
"[Your Name]" people search
"[Your Name]" public records
"[Your Name]" [State] records
```

Also search your **phone number** and **email address** in quotes; brokers expose
both on listing pages more often than you'd think.

For each result, open the page and copy its **URL** into your tracking sheet. You
don't need to read your full "report" — the URL and the site name is all you need
to file a removal.

> Tip: order the work. Start with the **most-visited, top-ranked** sites (they're
> the ones people actually see), then the long tail. 10 good removals beat 40
> half-finished ones.

## Step 2 — Work the broker's own opt-out lane (15–20 min each)

The consistent pattern for almost every broker (full list + links in
`05-opt-out-pages.md`):

1. Go to the broker's site and find **"Privacy" / "Do Not Sell My Info" / "Opt Out"
   / "Suppress" / "Remove my listing"** in the footer.
2. You'll usually be asked for:
   - Your listing **URL** (you have it), and/or
   - Name + city so their system finds the profile, and/or
   - An **email to receive a confirmation link** (use your project email).
3. Click the confirmation link when it lands. **The request isn't "done" until
   you've clicked that link.** This is the #1 reason opt-outs stall.
4. Mark "requested" in your sheet. Mark "completed" only after the confirmation
   says processed (often instant, sometimes "up to 72 hours").

**If there is no web form:** use the email template in `04-request-templates.md`,
sent to the broker's privacy address (usually `privacy@…` or listed in their
privacy policy — always confirm the exact address on the site itself).

## Step 3 — Handle the stubborn ones

- If a site replies "this is public record": resend using the **firm template**
  (still `04-request-templates.md`) — ask specifically for *suppression of the
  display of your profile* — most big people-search sites do suppress on request
  even for public-record data.
- If a site simply never replies: check for a state-law angle
  (`02-state-removal-rights.md`), then use **Permission Slip** (free, from Consumer
  Reports) to file the request on your behalf, and consider a complaint to your
  state AG or the FTC.

## Step 4 — The big platforms (do these once, they're high-leverage)

- **Google — "Results about you."** support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076 —
  lets you request removal of pages with your phone number, address, or ID from
  search results. Also its generic "Remove personal information from Google" form.
- **Bing** has a similar "Report a concern / removal of personal data" form.
- **Fraud-risk lane:** freeze your credit at all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian,
  TransUnion) — free, and it shrinks the value of leaked broker data.
- **Prescreened offers:** optoutprescreen.com (5 years or permanent).

## Step 5 — Log it, schedule it, stop for today

When you finish this pass:

- [ ] Update your tracking sheet with dates + confirmation numbers
- [ ] Add the **renewal reminders** from `06-renewals-calendar.md`
      (you'll re-check and re-submit every ~3–4 months for the first year)
- [ ] Do the "stay off" hardening from `07-stay-off-going-forward.md`

That's the whole loop: **find → request → confirm → log → re-check later.**
Everything else in this repo is detail supporting these five steps.
