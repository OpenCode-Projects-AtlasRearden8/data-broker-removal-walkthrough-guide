# Which States Give You Removal Rights

*Education only — not legal advice. Laws change; verify current details before relying
on them. Good neutral trackers: the IAPP state privacy-law tracker and your state
Attorney General's consumer-protection page.*

## The three big "buckets" of rights

### Bucket 1: Deletion / opt-out of sale or sharing (state privacy laws)

As of late 2026, these U.S. states have comprehensive consumer-privacy laws that
commonly include the **right to delete** your personal data and/or **opt out of
its sale or sharing** (some states apply the rights more broadly than others —
for example, some also let you opt out of *targeted advertising* even when the
data isn't technically "sold"):

- California (CCPA/CPRA — the strongest set, and applies to for-profit businesses
  over thresholds, including data brokers)
- Colorado
- Connecticut
- Virginia
- Utah
- Texas
- Oregon
- Montana
- Florida (limited — applies mainly to large platforms and includes an opt-out
  of sale / targeted ads, not a full deletion right for everyone)
- Delaware
- Iowa (narrower — no full deletion right, but opt-outs)
- Nebraska
- New Hampshire
- New Jersey
- Tennessee
- Minnesota
- Maryland (effective Oct 2025)
- Indiana (effective Jan 1, 2026 — relevant since Asdale Dynamics is an Indiana LLC)
- Additional states have laws scheduled or amended; **check a current tracker**.

What this means in practice: if you're a resident of one of these states, when you
email a broker you can invoke your statutory right ("I am a resident of [state] and
I request deletion of my personal data under [state]'s privacy law"). That language
carries weight and companies process it through legal-mandated pipelines — usually
faster and more reliably than a generic "please remove me" note.

### Bucket 2: Rights you have no matter where you live (federal)

- **FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act).** If a site sells consumer reports, it must
  follow reasonable-procedures rules and honor certain disputes. Employment/tenant
  background companies (like LexisNexis Risk, TransUnion-owned data resellers) have
  formal self-disclosure and opt-out processes.
- **DNC / opt-out notices.** You can stop prescreened credit/insurance offers for
  5 years or forever at the official **OptOutPrescreen.com** (run by the credit
  bureaus under FCRA §615(d)).
- **Do Not Call** registry (donotcall.gov) — stops many cold calls, but is separate
  from data-selling.
- **GDPR-style requests.** If you are an EU/UK resident (or one large international
  broker simply decides to be consistent worldwide), some deletion rights carry over
  and top brokers honor them globally. Worth trying if a site is based abroad.

### Bucket 3: Niche state programs worth knowing

- **Vermont and California** require commercial data brokers to register with the
  state. California's registry (maintained by the California Privacy Protection
  Agency) is **publicly searchable** — you can use it to find broker names and
  contact info for removal requests.
- Some state AGs (e.g., California, Texas) have gone after brokers publicly; you
  can file complaints with your state AG if a broker ignores a valid request.

## How to tell which bucket you get to use

1. Find your state in the list above (or check a tracker).
2. If your state appears: use the **state-specific template** in
   `04-request-templates.md` and name the law right in the email.
3. If your state doesn't appear (yet): use the **generic template**. It still works —
   most major brokers honor all US residents' opt-out requests anyway to keep things
   simple for themselves and to comply in the strictest states (progressive
   "nationwide enforcement" by the brokers isn't guaranteed, but it is the norm for
   the major people-search sites).
4. In either case: **send to the broker's own privacy/opt-out channel first** (see
   `05-opt-out-pages.md`). If it stalls, escalate (see `07-stay-off-going-forward.md`).

## A note of honesty about gray areas

- "Public record" is a mushy category. Courts have generally allowed republishing
  of genuinely public data, so removal requests sometimes get answered with
  "this is public record, we can't remove it." In practice, many of the big
  people-search sites honor removals *anyway* when asked through the right lane —
  which is exactly what this guide walks you through.
- Some brokers remove only the **profile page**, keeping the underlying record.
  The template in `04-request-templates.md` asks for both, in the right words.
