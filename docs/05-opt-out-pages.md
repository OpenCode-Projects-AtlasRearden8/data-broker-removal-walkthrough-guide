# Opt-Out Pages for Major Brokers (with links)

*Education only. We do not scrape or automate these sites — you visit each opt-out
page yourself, once, like any normal visitor.*

**Important:** brokers reorganize their privacy pages often. Before using any link
below, confirm you're on the company's **official domain** — the safest habit is:
*google "[broker name] opt out" and click only a URL within the broker's own
website.* Treat this table as a starting map, not gospel. Last checked: writing
time (2026).

## How you'll use this

1. Find the broker in the table.
2. Open the opt-out/suppression page (or its Privacy page → "Do Not Sell My
   Information"/"Opt Out" link in the site footer).
3. Paste the profile URL or search your name+city on their form.
4. Click the confirmation email link. Mark done in your tracker.

## Risk / identity companies (higher-impact, formal process)

- **LexisNexis Risk Solutions** — a formal "self-inquiry/opt-out" process exists;
  search "LexisNexis full file disclosure / opt out" from their official site.
  It's more paperwork, but it's the data feeding banks/insurers/landlords.
- **Thomson Reuters CLEAR** — request via their privacy office ("Subject Access
  / data privacy request" on thomsonreuters.com).

## The big search engines (high leverage, one-time)

- **Google "Results about you"** — support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076 —
  request removal of personal contact info from Google results. Also works via
  Google's "Remove personal information" form on any result.
- **Bing** — "Removal of personal data" report form on bing.com/microsoft support.
- **Yahoo** — privacy/scoop removal form via their help site.

## The long tail (sites not on any list)

When you find a broker not in this table:

1. Google **"[site name] opt out"** — click a result on the site's own domain.
2. No opt-out page? Open their **Privacy Policy** (footer) and find the
   "contact us about your data" address — often `privacy@domain`.
3. Use Template 1 or 2 from `04-request-templates.md`.
4. If the site is dodgy (no privacy policy, contact, or company info): that's a
   sign it won't honor requests — file a Google "Results about you" removal and
   a report to the BBB/your AG instead of wrestling with it forever.

## What about paid removal services (DeleteMe, Incogni, etc.)?

They exist and can save time; this guide deliberately covers the **free, manual**
path so you understand the process and can choose. If you use one anyway, still
do the checklist here for the sites they don't cover, and keep your renewal
calendar — remnants and re-acquisitions happen with every approach.
