# Keeping Your Data Off Going Forward

*Removal cleans the past. This page keeps the future tidy. Education only,
not legal or security advice.*

## Shrink the inflow — the honest one-time wins

In rough order of value-per-minute:

1. **Freeze credit at all three bureaus** (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Free,
   reversible with a PIN you control, and it starves the "I can open an account
   in your name" contour of leaked data.
2. **OptOutPrescreen.com** — stop prescreened credit/insurance offers for 5 years
   or permanently (official, FCRA-based). Fewer offers = fewer paper trails.
3. **donotcall.gov** — register your numbers; takes effect in ~31 days.
4. **Opt out of all marketing mail where you can** — DMAoptout (Mail Preference
   Service) for national lists; also individually decline catalogs' "share my
   data" boxes when you remember.
5. **Say no to "loyalty" surveys that ask too much.** "Birth month? Income band?
   How many people in the home?" — skip answers that are pure profiling bait.
   Loyalty discount ≠ obligation to deliver a dossier.

## Control what you publish yourself

- **Email aliases** (free in Gmail via `+tag`, or SimpleLogin/Duck addresses)
  for every sketchy sign-up. If an alias starts being sold, you know which form
  leaked it, and you can shut just that alias off.
- **Two phone numbers** if feasible: a real one for family/bank, a cheap VoIP
  number for forms, deliveries, and resale apps. This single habit kills more
  spam than any app.
- **A "burner identity" address** — P.O. box or UPS store address for anything
  that might get re-shared (marketplace sales, subscriptions, contest forms,
  even some deliveries). Keep your home address out of the commercial chain.
- **Username hygiene.** Search your old usernames; they link profiles across
  sites. Prefer unique, unlinked handles for new accounts.
- **Social check:** on any social profile you use, confirm your home city is
  off the bio, your relatives' full names aren't public, and old photo captions
  don't include addresses. Brokers scrape social pages too.

## For small-business / LLC owners (extra exposure)

Your business's registered agent address, officers' names, and status are
public by design — that's normal. What you *can* control:

- Use a **registered-agent service** rather than your home address as the agent;
  check whether your state lets you update the agent of record.
- Avoid listing your home address on the business's public-facing pages,
  invoices, or WHOIS/domain records (WHOIS privacy is on by default with most
  registrars; pay for it if not).
- Note that this affects your *business* identity, which cannot be fully
  unpublished — but it can be separated from your home identity.

## A basic monitoring habit (10 min/quarter)

- [ ] Search your name + city in incognito, page 1–2: any *new* listing?
- [ ] Search your phone number and main email in quotes
- [ ] Glance at haveibeenpwned.com for new breach exposure of your main email
- [ ] If anything new: run doc 03 Steps 2–3 on just the new entries

## If it still leaks: the escalation ladder

1. Broker's own opt-out again (sometimes a second pass works after data refreshes)
2. State privacy law template (doc 02/04)
3. Permission Slip (Consumer Reports, free) files the request for you
4. State AG complaint + FTC report — dated, attached, everything you've sent
5. For truly egregious cases (impersonation, stalking risk): consider a consult
   with a consumer-rights attorney or, if you're in a high-risk situation
   (stalking, doxxing, DV), a specialist resource such as the National DV
   hotline's tech-safety guidance or a qualified privacy attorney. Safety first:
   if a listing could threaten your physical safety, prioritize the major
   people-search sites and the Google "Results about you" removal *same day*,
   and treat everything else as secondary.

## The mindset, stated kindly

You will never be perfectly invisible — public records exist for good civic
reasons, and a nurse's license, an LLC filing, and a deed are all legitimately
public. The goal isn't zero footprint; it's **low value for sale, low noise in
search, and an easy annual routine**. This guide gets you there in a few focused
sittings plus a 60-minute routine once a year.
